# ICP Synopsis

Primary ICP:

- Estate Planning law firms in the United States
- roughly 3 to 25 staff members
- firms with enough volume to feel operational drag
- founder-led or admin-led firms frustrated by workflow inconsistency

Best-fit buyer profiles:

- owner/attorney who feels delivery chaos and cannot see bottlenecks
- firm administrator or operations lead responsible for keeping matters moving
- managing attorney who wants team accountability without constant chasing

Core firm signals:

- meaningful matter volume
- repeatable process across intake, drafting, signing, and post-signing
- multiple handoffs across team members
- current dependence on spreadsheets, task lists, tribal knowledge, or generic CRMs

Urgent pains:

- unclear ownership across stages
- critical dates tracked manually
- stale matters that sit untouched
- no clean visibility into open matters and overdue work
- weak accountability across team members
- no reliable timing data across Estate Planning delivery stages

Why Estate Planning first:

- PallmLaw is most mature here today
- the workflow is structured and repeatable
- the product already supports retained-rate and critical-date intelligence relevant to this practice
- the buyer pain is concrete and measurable

Adjacent delivered workflow support:

- Probate, Probate Release, and Trust Administration are valid product capabilities
- treat these as expansion and credibility signals, not a reason to broaden the primary ICP prematurely

Disqualifiers:

- solo firms with low matter volume and little handoff complexity
- firms mainly seeking a cheap task list or simple CRM replacement
- buyers expecting a generic AI chatbot solution rather than operational infrastructure
