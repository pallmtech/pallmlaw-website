# LawOps Marketing System

## Positioning

LawOps should be marketed as the operating system for law firms, with Estate Planning as the current wedge.

The strongest truthful story today is:

- workflow control across practice area, stage, critical date, and task
- firm intelligence that reveals operational reality
- accountability and visibility across the team
- firm-owned operational infrastructure rather than generic rented SaaS

## Current ICP

Primary ICP:

- U.S. Estate Planning firms
- 3 to 25 staff
- repeated workflow across intake, retained, design, drafting, signing, and binder/post-signing work
- enough volume that bottlenecks and overdue tasks create real management pain

Likely buyers:

- founder / owner attorney
- managing attorney
- firm administrator
- operations lead

## Why this ICP fits now

- the product is most mature in Estate Planning
- the intelligence layer already supports Estate Planning retained-rate and critical-date timing metrics
- the workflow is structured enough to show obvious before/after value

## Messaging spine

Primary line:

- Bring ownership, consistency, and visibility to legal workflow.

Supporting lines:

- Stop managing your firm by memory.
- Replace handoff chaos with operational control.
- See open matters, stale work, overdue tasks, and team reliability in one system.
- Build workflow infrastructure your firm owns.

## Social media content pillars

1. Operational pain
- example: "Most firms do not have a people problem. They have a handoff problem."

2. Visibility
- example: "If you have to ask where a matter stands, your system is already failing."

3. Accountability
- example: "A stage without an owner is just a delay waiting to happen."

4. Estate Planning specificity
- example: "Retained to Design. Design to Drafting. Drafting to Signing. If you cannot measure those transitions, you cannot improve them."

5. Anti-buzzword
- example: "AI on top of workflow chaos just helps you misunderstand your firm faster."

## Founder-led social post examples

1.
Most firms do not have a productivity problem.
They have a visibility problem.

If you cannot see:

- what is open
- what is overdue
- what is stale
- where the bottleneck lives

then you are not managing a workflow.
You are managing updates.

2.
AI is not the first fix for a law firm.

First:
- define the stages
- define the critical dates
- define the tasks
- define the owner

Then intelligence becomes useful.

Without that structure, "smart" software just gives you smarter confusion.

3.
In Estate Planning, the handoff is where margin disappears.

Retained to design.
Design to drafting.
Drafting to signing.
Signing to binder.

## Operating rule

When writing anything for LawOps, avoid generic legal tech language.
The copy should sound like it came from someone who has seen workflow break inside a real firm.
